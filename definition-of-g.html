Definition of G

Created by Shaimaa Said Soltan

One definition, two readings. As a pull: the force between two 1 kg masses 1 m apart. As an orbit: the orbit balance that 1 kg of mass produces at 1 m. Change the masses and distances below; the force and the orbit change, but G stays the same.

G is the pull between two 1 kg masses whose centres are 1 m apart. Equivalently, it is the orbit balance that 1 kg of mass produces at 1 m.
6.674×10⁻¹¹ N per (kg²/m²) = m³/s² per kg = (2π / 8.9 days)² × 1 m³/kg

Reading 1: the unit pull

Two masses, the point P where r₁ and r₂ meet (the mass rule r₁/r₂ = m₁/m₂ with r₁·r₂ = r²), and the pull between them.

Reading 2: the unit orbit

A speck circling a ball of mass M at distance a. Time runs at 1 day per half second.

Every real system is a scaled copy

G from each orbit's observed size and period alone: K = 4π²a³/T², then G = K/M. The unit pair is the definition; the others are measurements.

Every row gives the same G to within about 0.3%; the Moon's orbit is slightly elliptical. The pull F ranges over 34 powers of ten, from 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N to 4.16×10²³ N, while G stays fixed. The unit-pair period, 6.3 days, is shorter than the 8.9 days of a speck because both 1 kg masses pull (M = 2 kg).
